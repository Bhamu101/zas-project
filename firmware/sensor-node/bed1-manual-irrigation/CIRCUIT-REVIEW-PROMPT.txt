I need help diagnosing a low-voltage DC control circuit for a solar-powered
drip irrigation system. Act as an electronics engineer reviewing my wiring
and multimeter readings to find the fault.

SYSTEM CONTEXT
--------------
An ESP32 microcontroller drives a 12V DC water pump and a 12V solenoid
valve for one irrigation bed. The pump draws too much current (~15A) for
the ESP32 to switch directly, so a 2N2222 NPN transistor is used as a
low-side pilot switch to energize a 12V automotive relay's coil, and that
relay's high-current contacts carry the actual pump power. The valve is on
a separate, standard 2-channel 5V relay module (unrelated to the fault,
included for completeness).

PARTS IN THIS CIRCUIT
----------------------
- ESP32 DevKit (GPIO18 drives the pump circuit, 3.3V logic)
- 1x 2N2222 NPN transistor (TO-92 package)
- 1x 220 ohm resistor (base current limiting)
- 1x 1N4007 diode (flyback protection across the relay coil)
- 1x automotive relay, 12V coil, rated >=30A contacts, standard 5-pin
  (30 = common/power-in, 87 = normally-open output, 87a = normally-closed,
  unpopulated on this specific relay, 85/86 = coil)
- 1x DC submersible pump, 12V, ~15A draw
- 1x fused +12V DC bus (20A inline fuse), from a 12V battery

INTENDED WIRING
----------------
ESP32 GPIO18 -> 220 ohm resistor -> 2N2222 Base
2N2222 Emitter -> common ground
2N2222 Collector -> relay pin 85 (coil "-" / low side)
Relay pin 86 (coil "+") -> fused +12V bus (this leg is ALWAYS live,
  independent of the transistor)
1N4007 flyback diode across the coil: cathode (banded end) -> pin 86,
  anode -> pin 85 / Collector junction
Relay pin 30 (high-current input) -> fused +12V bus (separate wire from
  pin 86, same bus)
Relay pin 87 (switched output) -> pump red (+) lead
Pump blue (-) lead -> common ground

Firmware logic: GPIO18 HIGH should saturate the transistor, pulling the
collector (and therefore relay pin 85) down toward 0V, which creates
~12V across the coil (pin 86 minus pin 85) and energizes the relay,
closing the connection between pin 30 and pin 87 to power the pump.
GPIO18 LOW should leave the transistor fully off, so no current flows
through the coil at all.

SYMPTOM
-------
When triggered, the relay makes an audible click, but the pump never
actually runs. Voltage checks with a multimeter, referenced to common
ground unless noted:

- Idle (GPIO18 LOW, not triggered):
  - Relay pin 86 to ground: 12.9V (matches the fused bus, as expected)
  - Relay pin 85 to ground: 6.0V (expected: should be close to 12.9V too,
    since with the transistor fully off there should be no current
    through the coil and therefore no voltage drop across it)
  - Between pin 85 and pin 86 directly: 5.6V drop
- Triggered (GPIO18 HIGH, cycle running):
  - Relay pin 30 to ground: 12V (correct, bus power present)
  - Relay pin 87 to ground: 0V (expected: should read close to 12V,
    since the relay should be switching pin 30's power through to pin 87)

So: pin 86 has correct bus voltage, pin 30 has correct bus voltage, but
the relay never actually switches pin 30 through to pin 87, and there's
clearly a live current path through the coil even when the transistor
should be off (the 85-86 voltage drop at idle).

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
---------------------
1. Given these exact readings, what is the most likely root cause? (My own

   swapped from the intended wiring above -- e.g. Base/Collector/Emitter
   mixed up, since TO-92 pinout varies by manufacturer and I did not
   verify the actual part against its datasheet before wiring it; or the
   diode is reversed, which — backwards — creates a leakage/short path
   across the coil instead of protecting it. Tell me if you agree, disagree,
   or see another explanation I'm missing.)
2. What specific multimeter tests (in diode-test mode, with the components

3. Is there anything else in the "intended wiring" above that looks wrong
